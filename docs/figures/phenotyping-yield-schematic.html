Supplementary Figure S5 · phenotyping yield

Why a 12-marker panel commits only a fifth of its cells: abstention is a conjunction, the confidence level is non-monotonic, and the classifier is right about the cells it keeps

S1 · pipeline S2 · registration S3 · quality control S4 · failure semantics S5 · phenotyping yield S6 · export contract

A low classification rate reads like a data problem. It is not. The phenotyper resolves each marker independently into one of four signs, and a cell is committed only when enough of those signs land confidently — a conjunction over 12 markers, so per-marker confidence multiplies. On a simulated cohort with known ground truth and clean separation, the shipped panel commits 6.5% of cells while being 94.7% accurate on the ones it does commit. The missing cells are not misclassified; they are abstained. Three levers recover most of them, and one of the three moves in the opposite direction to the obvious guess.

defaults: pheno_alpha = 0.05 · pheno_min_cal = 50 · pheno_max_enumerate = 100000 · panel_spec = null · panel_model = null
a

The expectation: more markers, sharper calls

The panel declares 12 lineage markers that split the tree and 7 state markers that are recorded but never affect the call. Biology prunes the cube hard — and that pruning is the part that works.

Patterns are named by specificity, so many patterns collapse onto one phenotype name. That collapse is deliberate and it helps.

The trap. Constraint pruning is not the bottleneck — it is doing its job. The bottleneck is upstream of F: whether each marker's sign is known for a given cell. Adding a marker to sharpen the tree also adds one more sign that has to come back confident, and those requirements multiply.
b

The atom: two one-sided conformal tests per marker, per cell

Each marker gets two p-values against its own calibration set — p_neg (is this cell unusually high for a negative?) and p_pos (unusually low for a positive?). A hypothesis stays in the prediction set while its p-value exceeds α. The size of the surviving set is the sign.

sign prediction set effect on the pattern match
pos{pos} requires pattern value 1 — a real constraint
neg{neg} requires pattern value 0 — a real constraint
free{pos, neg} matches any value — constrains nothing
contra{ } forces no match at all → Artefact
α is a two-sided knob, and both sides bite. Lowering α widens both prediction sets, pushing cells into free. Raising it narrows both, and once both hypotheses are rejected the cell is contra. The two failure modes sit on opposite corners of the same square, which is why the yield curve in panel e has a peak rather than a slope.

c

Why one undetermined marker can cost the whole call

A free marker does not narrow the subcube, so the cell keeps every feasible pattern that agrees with its known signs. Commitment needs the survivors to share a single name — not a single pattern.

absorbed — free costs nothing

A CD45− cell: requires already forces CD3, CD56, CD14 (and everything below them) to 0. Those markers may be free and it does not matter — F pins them anyway.

CD45 neg · SMA neg · PANCK pos · CD3 free · CD8 free · FOXP3 free · GZMB free
survivors all named PANCK_Tumor → committed
not absorbed — free is fatal

A CD45+ CD3+ cell with CD8, CD4 and FOXP3 undetermined spans four different T-cell leaves. Nothing in F can pick between them.

CD45 pos · CD3 pos · CD8 free · CD4 free · FOXP3 free
survivors named T_helper, CD4_Treg, T_cytotoxic, CD8_Treg → Ambiguous
The root gate is a single point of failure. If CD45 itself is free, the cell straddles the immune and non-immune arms simultaneously and is Ambiguous no matter how clean every other marker is. In the measured run below, CD45 was free for 93% of cells.

Median cell: 10 of 12 lineage markers undetermined. The conjunction is the whole story — at a per-marker confidence of q, a cell needing k decisive markers commits with probability ≈ qk.

Read this before tuning anything. High accuracy with low yield means the machinery is working and the operating point is wrong. A pipeline that misclassified cells would need a different fix entirely — check phenotype_qc.json → chosen_alpha and degraded_markers first, because those two fields separate "wrong operating point" from "broken calibration".
e

The counterintuitive part: yield peaks, then collapses

Loosening α converts free into decided signs — until it starts converting decided signs into contra. Past the peak, cells stop being Ambiguous and start being Artefact, which is worse: an Ambiguous cell still carries a candidate list, an Artefact carries nothing.

Accuracy falls as yield rises. 94.7% → 82.7% between α = 0.01 and 0.05. This is a precision/recall dial, not a free lunch — the right setting depends on whether a downstream analysis would rather have 51% of cells at 83% purity or 6.5% at 95%. State the choice in the methods; do not let it be chosen implicitly.
f

You did not pick α — conformal risk control did, from one marker pair

pheno_alpha is not the level that gets used. It is a risk target. When the panel declares any audited constraint, the run scans a fixed grid and keeps the largest α whose upper confidence bound on excess co-positivity clears that target.

the mechanism
grid 0.005 · 0.01 · 0.02 · 0.05 · 0.1 · 0.2 UCB R(α) + √(ln(1/δ) / 2n(α)), δ = 0.1

The bound is not monotone: n(α), the committed-cell count, grows with α, so the width term shrinks while the risk term grows. The qualifying set is an interior interval, which is why the scan is exhaustive rather than a binary search.

The fallback is the most conservative value, not the target. If no grid value qualifies, α becomes grid[0] = 0.005 — ten times stricter than the 0.05 you set, and the run says so only in phenotype_qc.json.
what it was calibrated against

Rare and soft exclusivity pairs are two-coloured into enforce and audit so that nothing is both penalised and used as an unbiased check. Correct in principle — but on this panel it leaves a single audited pair.

g

Four improvements, measured where possible

Ordered by yield per unit of effort. The first is the large one and it changes no statistics at all — it only changes what is reported when the candidates disagree.

1 · report the lowest common ancestor instead of "Ambiguous"

Every candidate in the example is an immune cell. The classifier already knows that with high confidence; it throws the knowledge away because it cannot pick the leaf. Walk the parent chain the panel already declares and emit the deepest node all candidates share.

αnow with LCA LCA correct
0.016.5% 28.1%98.1%
0.0215.6% 42.9%96.4%
0.0551.3% 63.4%93.9%

This is what TRACERx-PHLEX's TYPEx does when a cell is unassigned: fall back to the high-confidence major lineage. It reports cutting Unassigned/Ambiguous from 44% to 4.7%.

2 · floor the CRC search

Let the risk target lower α, but not below a stated floor — or drop to reporting mode and use pheno_alpha directly when the audit rests on a single pair. Also widen the audit set: a risk estimate built on one marker pair is a thin basis for a global decision.

measured 6.5% → 51.3% at α = 0.05
3 · give markers real anchors

15 of 19 markers compile with an unresolved reference and fall back to a GMM on their own values, so the calibration is self-referential — the cells being tested also define the null. Declaring negative_reference / positive_reference for the gate markers (as the panel already does for GZMB, L1CAM, P53, PDL1, ARID1A) sharpens the p-values that everything else rests on.

not measured here — panel authoring change
4 · fix the signal, not the statistics

Lateral spillover in dense tissue is what makes modes overlap in the first place. Compensating it at the segmentation boundary (REDSEA-style, using the shared perimeter between adjacent cells) raises per-marker separation, which raises every marker's confidence and therefore compounds across the conjunction.

the only fix that raises yield and accuracy together
Already exported, already usable. Every cell carries pheno_score:<Name> — a softmax over its candidate patterns — plus p_neg:/p_pos: per marker and the candidate list. An Ambiguous cell is not an empty row. Downstream analysis can threshold the soft scores itself without any change to the pipeline.

i

What this figure does not claim

The honest boundary of the measurement above.

The numbers are from simulation, not tissue. Log-normal modes separated by 2.5σ, spatially uniform, no spillover, no segmentation error, no batch effect. Real data will sit lower on every curve. The shape of the α response and the LCA gain are structural and should transfer; the absolute percentages should not be quoted as expected yield.
The LCA fallback is not implemented. Panel g measures it as a post-hoc analysis over the real classifier's candidate lists. It is not in classify.py, and adding it needs a decision about how a coarse call is represented downstream so an Immune row is never mistaken for a leaf call.
No spatial information is used. Each cell is classified independently. Methods that borrow strength from neighbours (CELESTA, STELLAR) resolve exactly the marginal cells that end up Ambiguous here — but adopting one is a different architecture, not a tuning change.
A degraded marker fails quietly toward abstention. Both p-values are forced to 1.0, so the marker is free for every cell at every α and the yield loss looks exactly like a bad operating point. Only degraded_markers in the QC JSON distinguishes them.

Supplementary Figure S5 — where phenotyping yield goes. (a) Constraint pruning takes the 212 lineage cube to 104 feasible patterns over 13 named phenotypes, and is not the bottleneck. (b) Each marker is resolved independently into pos/neg/free/contra by two one-sided conformal p-values thresholded at α; free and contra occupy opposite corners of that decision square, which is the origin of the non-monotonicity in (e). (c) A free marker constrains nothing, so a cell commits only when the surviving patterns share one phenotype name; the constraint graph absorbs free markers below a closed gate but cannot absorb them across sibling leaves, and an undetermined root gate is unrecoverable. (d) On 20,000 simulated cells with known truth the shipped panel commits 6.5% at 94.7% accuracy, with a median of 10 of 12 markers undetermined — the loss is abstention, not error. (e) Yield against α peaks near 0.05–0.1 and collapses beyond it as cells convert from Ambiguous to Artefact, while accuracy falls monotonically: a precision/recall dial that must be chosen deliberately. (f) The applied α is selected by conformal risk control over a fixed grid, falling back to the most conservative grid value when no candidate clears the bound; here a single audited marker pair set the level for all twelve markers. (g) Four improvements, the largest of which reports the deepest common ancestor of the candidate set instead of discarding the cell. (h) The governing parameters and the three QC fields that separate a wrong operating point from broken calibration. (i) Limits: the measurements are simulated, the ancestor fallback is analysed but not implemented, no spatial context is used, and a degraded marker is indistinguishable from a bad α without the QC JSON.
